Plan: step 1 is a provider-agnostic `CoffinAI` with an offline template generator and a fallback wrapper (no network, no keys); step 2 adds a tiny proxy (e.g. a Cloudflare Worker) that holds the key with a rate limit and spend cap, falling back to offline if it's down.

## Day 17 · Nov 24, 2026 (a few days early) — graveyard-seance specced
- **Thirteenth spec: `graveyard-seance`.** Hold a séance for any grave: a revival score (0–100, in three bands: Restless, Stirring, Ready to rise), three concrete steps to bring the project back, and one spooky verdict line. CoffinAI already had a `seanceVerdict` purpose from Day 7, so this spec needs **no change to coffin_core**: the score and steps are pure rules in Graveyard, and CoffinAI only writes the verdict line, offline, no key.
- **The worked examples caught a design problem before any code.** I asked for a table of real graves. The arithmetic was right, but the balance was wrong for the people this app is for: "Ready to rise" needed a project dead under a month *and* ~16,000 stars, a typical side project (10 stars, dead 6 months) scored "Restless", and a grave buried by hand could never pass 45. Every user would have got the same gloomy verdict. Rebalanced so recency carries most of the score, stars help a little, and a hand-buried grave gets the same "you remember it" bonus as a described repo. Now: a 1-month-old side project is Ready to rise, 6 months is Stirring, atom/atom (archived) is Restless, flutter/flutter is Ready to rise. I checked all eight rows by hand.
